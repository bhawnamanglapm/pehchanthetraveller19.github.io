A Week of Not Booking Anything

An experiment in arriving in Hoi An with a bicycle, no reservations and no plan.

Pehchan Editorial2025-05-065 min read
  1. Home
  2. Stories
  3. A Week of Not Booking Anything

The rule was one booking: seven nights in a twelve-room house on the wrong side of the river. Everything else had to be decided that morning, on a bicycle, in a town small enough that this is not reckless.

What happens on day two

You get bored, and boredom is the mechanism. Without a schedule I cycled to the herb village because it was the direction I had not been in, ended up in a conversation about basil varieties with a woman who has grown them her whole life, and stayed for two hours. No tour would have allocated two hours to basil.

You get bored, and boredom is the mechanism.

The things you should still book

Cooking classes fill. Tailors need fitting appointments and lead time. The lantern full-moon nights fill every room in town. Unbooking works for the middle of a trip, not for the edges and not for the things with capacity limits.

Where it fails

It fails in high season, it fails anywhere with a permit system, and it fails if you have four days rather than eight. It also fails if you are the kind of traveller who will spend the unplanned morning on your phone researching what you should be doing — in which case book the trip and enjoy it, which is a legitimate way to travel and not a lesser one.

But in a flat town with a bicycle and a week, it produced the best trip I had that year, and the only itinerary I could write for it afterwards would have ruined it.

Hoi An guide
