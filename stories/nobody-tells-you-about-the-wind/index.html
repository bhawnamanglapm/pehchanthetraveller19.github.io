Nobody Tells You About the Wind

Patagonia in photographs is still. Patagonia in person is not, and it changes everything about how you plan.

Pehchan Editorial2025-07-306 min read
  1. Home
  2. Stories
  3. Nobody Tells You About the Wind

Four days in El Chaltén and I saw Fitz Roy for about ninety minutes total. That is not a complaint; it is the base rate. The people who come back with the photograph are the ones who stayed long enough to be there on the right morning.

Plan for the weather window, not the itinerary

Everywhere else you plan a route and hope the weather cooperates. Here you plan a set of options and pick one at six in the morning based on the forecast. The lodge posts it in the common room. Everyone reads it at breakfast and the village reorganises itself accordingly.

Which means a four-day stay is not four days of walking. It is two or three days of walking and one or two of reading, and if you have booked a tight schedule you will burn a weather window sitting on a bus.

You plan a set of options and pick one at six in the morning.

The wind is a real hazard, not an atmosphere

On the final section up to Laguna de los Tres, gusts come over the ridge with enough force to move you sideways. People turn back there, and they are right to. The mountain is not going anywhere and the trail is free — you can come back tomorrow, which is the underrated advantage of a place that requires no permit.

What to do with a blown-out day

Walk to Chorrillo del Salto, which takes an hour and is sheltered. Or do the lower Laguna Capri section and turn around at the treeline. Or, genuinely, do nothing: El Chaltén has a bakery, three good places to eat, and a common room culture built around people waiting for weather. Some of the best conversations I had on that trip happened on the days I did not walk.
