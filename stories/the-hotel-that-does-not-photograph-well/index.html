The Hotel That Does Not Photograph Well

Why the best places to stay are often the ones with no wide-angle shot, and how to find them.

Pehchan Editorial2025-08-216 min read
  1. Home
  2. Stories
  3. The Hotel That Does Not Photograph Well

The best room I have stayed in this year is four and a half tatami mats, has a window onto a courtyard the size of a parking space, and is impossible to photograph. There is nowhere to stand.

The wide-angle problem

Hotels are now designed for a camera at chest height in the corner of the room. That is a real design constraint and it produces real consequences: rooms that are wide rather than deep, beds facing the view rather than the wall, freestanding baths placed where they photograph rather than where you would want to sit in one.

A machiya townhouse cannot compete on this and does not try. Its pleasures are sequential — you come through a dark entrance, past a garden you did not expect, up a staircase that is too steep, into a room where the light comes through a wooden lattice and moves across the floor for about forty minutes in the late afternoon. None of that survives a single frame.

Its pleasures are sequential. None of that survives a single frame.

How to find them anyway

Read the one-star and three-star reviews rather than the five-star ones. The complaints tell you the shape of the place: 'stairs too steep', 'walls thin', 'no lift', 'small pool'. Those are the fingerprints of an old building that was converted rather than constructed, which is usually what you want.

Look at the room count. Under fifteen rooms means the person at the desk can actually book you a table. Over eighty and you are in a system.

And look for what the property does other than accommodate you. A working lemon terrace, a functioning tea estate, a family that has weaving looms in the back. The stay is better when the hotel is not the whole business.

The disclosure

We write about stays we would book ourselves, and we say plainly when a link earns us a commission. That does not make our judgement perfect. It should make it legible.
