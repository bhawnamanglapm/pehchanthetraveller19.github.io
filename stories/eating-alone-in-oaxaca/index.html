Eating Alone in Oaxaca

Nine days of solo meals in a city where food is a communal act, and what the market taught me about being on my own.

Pehchan Editorial2025-10-027 min read
  1. Home
  2. Stories
  3. Eating Alone in Oaxaca

The hardest table to ask for anywhere is a table for one at eight in the evening. In Oaxaca I stopped asking, and started eating standing up at the market, and the trip improved immediately.

The market solves the solo problem

Mercado 20 de Noviembre has a corridor of grills where you buy meat by weight, take it to a counter, and someone hands you tortillas and salsas without a conversation about whether anyone is joining you. You sit on a bench next to strangers doing exactly the same thing. Nobody is eating alone in a market. Everybody is eating alone in a market.

This is not a workaround. It is how a great deal of the city eats, and the food is not a lesser version of the restaurant food — in several cases it is the same cooks, cooking the thing they are best at, for a third of the money.

Seven moles is a marketing number

You will hear about the seven moles constantly. It is a real tradition and also a tidy figure that suits a menu. What I found more interesting was that every family cooks negro differently, and that asking about the difference is the single most reliable way to start a conversation in this city.

Nobody is eating alone in a market. Everybody is eating alone in a market.

On mezcal, and drinking alone

Mezcal is agricultural. An espadín takes six to eight years in the ground before anyone distils it; a tobalá can take considerably longer, and it does not grow in rows. Once you know that, the bar-shot version stops making sense and you start sipping it at room temperature, which is what everyone here does anyway.

A palenque day is also the easiest way to meet people if you have gone quiet. You share a van with six strangers, you all taste the same thing, and by the second stop somebody has opinions.

What nine days alone was actually like

Better than I expected, and boring in the middle, which I now think is the point. Days four and five of any solo trip are flat. You have exhausted the novelty and not yet arrived at the rhythm. If you can push through without booking a tour to fill the silence, day six is usually when the trip becomes yours.
