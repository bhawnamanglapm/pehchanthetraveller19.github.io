Hoi An

A preserved trading port where tailors, rice fields and an old town of ochre walls sit twenty minutes from an empty beach.

Why visit Hoi An

Hoi An survived the twentieth century almost intact because the river silted up and the trade moved elsewhere. What is left is a compact old town of Japanese, Chinese and Vietnamese merchant architecture, ringed by rice paddies and fishing villages that are still working rather than staged. It is also, quietly, one of the best food towns in Asia.

Best time to visit

  • February to April: dry, warm, and before the summer heat peaks.
  • May to August is hot but reliably sunny — good for the beach.
  • October to November is the wet season and the old town genuinely floods some years.
  • Lantern festival falls on the full moon of each lunar month and is worth planning around.

How to get there

  • Fly to Da Nang (DAD), then 45 minutes by car south along the coast.
  • The Hai Van Pass drive or train from Hue is one of the great coastal journeys in Asia.
  • Within town, cycle. The old town is pedestrianised for much of the day.

How many days do you need

3–4 days, or a week if you are using it as a slow-travel base.

Where to stay

Old town for atmosphere and noise, An Bang beach for space and sea, or the rice fields between the two for the best of both with a bicycle.

Best things to do in Hoi An

  • Cycle the back lanes to Tra Que herb village early, before the heat.
  • Commission one considered garment from a tailor rather than five rushed ones.
  • Take a basket-boat trip through the Cam Thanh water coconut palms.
  • Learn to cook cao lau properly — the noodle only exists here.
  • Spend an afternoon at An Bang beach with nothing scheduled.

Food

  • Cao lau — smoky pork, thick noodles, and water drawn from specific local wells.
  • Banh mi in Hoi An is a genuine local speciality, not a tourist import.
  • White rose dumplings, made by a handful of families.
  • Com ga Hoi An, the turmeric chicken rice.

Budget

One of the best value destinations in this guide. Excellent boutique stays sit at mid-range prices; luxury beach resorts anchor the top of the market.

Safety & practical information

  • Very safe; the main risks are traffic and the sea's rip currents in winter.
  • Motorbike rental requires the correct licence — check your travel insurance carefully.
  • Flooding in October and November can close the old town for days.

Local culture

  • Bargaining in the market is expected and should stay good-humoured.
  • Cover shoulders in the assembly halls and temples.
  • Many tailors are family businesses — a fitting appointment is a commitment.

Frequently asked questions

How many days should you spend in Hoi An?

Three days covers the old town, a beach day and a countryside cycle. Extend to a week if you want tailoring fittings or are travelling slowly.

Is Hoi An worth visiting in the rainy season?

October and November bring real flooding. Prices drop and the town is atmospheric, but plan flexible dates and stay outside the lowest streets.

Should you stay in the old town or at the beach?

The old town is convenient and lively but noisy at night. An Bang beach is calmer and only a fifteen-minute cycle away — most repeat visitors choose the beach or the rice fields.
