Chiang Mai

A walled old city in the northern hills — temple mornings, mountain afternoons and the best coffee culture in Southeast Asia.

Why visit Chiang Mai

Chiang Mai is the rare city that works equally well for a four-day visit and a four-month stay. The moated old city holds three hundred temples; an hour's drive gets you into Doi Inthanon's cloud forest or the coffee-growing hills around Mae Rim. It has also become the default base for long-stay remote workers, which has produced an unusually good café and food scene.

Best time to visit

  • November to February: cool, dry and the reason people fall in love with the north.
  • Loi Krathong and Yi Peng in November are extraordinary — book months ahead.
  • Avoid March and April: this is burning season, and the air quality is genuinely poor.
  • The green season from June to October is lush, quiet and good value.

How to get there

  • Chiang Mai International (CNX) sits fifteen minutes from the old city.
  • The overnight sleeper train from Bangkok is slow, scenic and a good experience in its own right.
  • Rent a scooter only with the correct licence; otherwise use the red songthaew trucks or ride-hailing.

How many days do you need

4 days for the city and one mountain day; 10+ if you are basing yourself here.

Where to stay

Inside the old city walls for temples and walkability, Nimmanhaemin for cafés and a younger scene, or Mae Rim in the hills for resort quiet.

Best things to do in Chiang Mai

  • Climb to Doi Suthep before sunrise and come down before the buses.
  • Visit an ethical elephant sanctuary that does not offer rides or bathing.
  • Drive the Samoeng loop through the coffee hills.
  • Take a northern Thai cooking class — the food here is not Bangkok food.
  • Spend a morning at the Sunday walking street, then leave before it fills.

Food

  • Khao soi — the northern curry noodle that defines the city.
  • Sai ua, the herb-heavy northern sausage.
  • Nam prik ong and sticky rice at a khantoke meal.
  • Third-wave coffee grown two hours away in the hills.

Budget

Extremely good value across every tier. Boutique guesthouses are inexpensive; hill resorts and wellness retreats sit in the premium band.

Safety & practical information

  • Safe and very accustomed to independent travellers, including solo women.
  • Air quality between February and April is a genuine health consideration.
  • Scooter accidents are the leading cause of traveller injury here — many insurers will not cover unlicensed riding.

Local culture

  • Temples require covered shoulders and knees for everyone.
  • Never touch someone's head, and avoid pointing your feet at people or Buddha images.
  • The north has its own Lanna identity, language and cuisine — it is not simply 'Thailand, further up'.

Frequently asked questions

When is the worst time to visit Chiang Mai?

March and April. Agricultural burning across the region regularly pushes air quality into unhealthy ranges, and the haze obscures the mountain views that are the main reason to be there.

Is Chiang Mai good for solo travellers?

It is one of the easiest cities in Asia to travel alone — walkable, inexpensive, safe, with a large long-stay community and plenty of group classes and treks to join.

How does Chiang Mai compare to Bangkok?

Smaller, cooler, slower and more nature-adjacent. Most travellers pair them: a few days in Bangkok for the city energy, then north for the hills.
