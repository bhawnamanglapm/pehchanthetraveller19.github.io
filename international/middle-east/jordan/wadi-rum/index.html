Wadi Rum

A protected desert of sandstone jebels and red sand where Bedouin camps have become one of travel's great overnight experiences.

Why visit Wadi Rum

Wadi Rum is a landscape at a scale that does not photograph well — mountains rising vertically out of flat sand, canyons that narrow to shoulder width, and silence at night that most travellers have never experienced. The protected area is managed by local Bedouin communities, which means visiting well and visiting responsibly are the same thing here.

Best time to visit

  • March to May and September to November are ideal — warm days, cold nights.
  • Winter nights drop below freezing; camps are heated but bring layers.
  • June to August is extremely hot, though desert nights remain manageable.
  • Sandstorms are possible in spring; they pass within a day.

How to get there

  • Fly to Aqaba (AQJ), an hour away, or Amman (AMM), around four hours.
  • Most travellers combine Petra, Wadi Rum and Aqaba in a single loop.
  • Vehicles stop at the visitor centre; camps collect guests in 4x4s from there.

How many days do you need

1–2 nights. One night is the minimum to justify the journey; two lets you go deeper into the protected area.

Where to stay

Bedouin camps range from simple goat-hair tents to bubble domes and designed desert suites. Location within the protected area matters more than the tier.

Best things to do in Wadi Rum

  • Take a 4x4 route to Khazali canyon and the Nabataean inscriptions.
  • Climb Jebel Burdah's rock bridge with a guide if you are comfortable with exposure.
  • Sit through sunset from a jebel rather than from camp.
  • Eat a zarb — meat and vegetables cooked in a buried sand oven.
  • Stay up for the stars; the darkness here is a genuine attraction.

Food

  • Zarb, the underground oven meal, is the signature.
  • Mansaf, Jordan's national dish of lamb and fermented yoghurt.
  • Bedouin tea with sage and far more sugar than you expect.
  • Simple mezze — camps cater; there are no restaurants in the protected area.

Budget

Camps span the full range from basic shared facilities to designed private suites. The Jordan Pass covers protected area entry and is usually worth buying.

Safety & practical information

  • Book through the official Wadi Rum protected area system or a reputable operator.
  • Temperature swings between day and night are extreme — pack layers even in summer.
  • Phone signal is patchy to absent; tell someone your camp name and route.

Local culture

  • Bedouin hospitality is real and generous; accept the tea.
  • Modest dress is appreciated, particularly for women, outside camp.
  • Alcohol is not served in most camps; ask rather than assume.

Frequently asked questions

How many nights should you spend in Wadi Rum?

One night works but feels rushed. Two nights lets you reach the further reaches of the protected area and spend an unhurried evening under the stars.

Is Wadi Rum suitable for families?

Yes. Camps are used to children, 4x4 tours are short, and the sand dunes are a natural playground. Choose a camp closer to the visitor centre for shorter transfers.

How cold does Wadi Rum get at night?

Winter nights regularly fall to freezing, and even spring and autumn nights are cold. Camps provide blankets, but bring warm layers regardless of season.
