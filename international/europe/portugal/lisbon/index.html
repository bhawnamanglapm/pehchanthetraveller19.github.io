Lisbon

Seven hills, Atlantic light and a city that has changed fast without losing its tiled, tram-rattled character.

Why visit Lisbon

Lisbon is a capital city with a coastal temperament: it faces west, the light is exceptional in the late afternoon, and the surf is thirty minutes away. It has changed dramatically in a decade — more visitors, higher prices, a serious food scene — but the miradouros, the tiles and the fado rooms of Alfama still carry the older city underneath.

Best time to visit

  • April to June and September to October: warm, clear and less crowded than midsummer.
  • July and August are hot and busy, though Atlantic evenings stay pleasant.
  • Winter is mild, rainy in bursts and the best value of the year.
  • June's Santo António festival takes over the whole city for a night.

How to get there

  • Lisbon Airport (LIS) is unusually close to the centre — twenty minutes by metro.
  • Excellent rail links to Porto in under three hours.
  • Metro, tram and walking cover the city; the hills are steeper than the map suggests.

How many days do you need

3–4 days for the city, 6–7 with Sintra and the coast.

Where to stay

Príncipe Real for design hotels and calm, Alfama for atmosphere and stairs, Chiado for central convenience, Belém for museums and space.

Best things to do in Lisbon

  • Ride tram 28 early morning, or walk its route instead and see more.
  • Spend a day in Sintra, arriving before the palaces open.
  • Eat your way through the Time Out market once, then go find the originals.
  • Book a small fado room in Alfama, not a dinner show.
  • Take the train to Cascais and walk the coast path.

Food

  • Pastéis de nata, and yes, the Belém originals do taste different.
  • Bacalhau in any of its many preparations.
  • Grilled sardines, but only really in season through summer.
  • Petiscos — the Portuguese answer to small plates — with vinho verde.

Budget

Still good value for a Western European capital, though hotel prices have risen sharply. Mid-range and boutique stays offer the strongest value.

Safety & practical information

  • Very safe; pickpocketing on tram 28 and in Baixa is the main nuisance.
  • Hills and cobbles are hard on knees and unsuitable for wheeled luggage.
  • Atlantic beaches have strong currents — swim where lifeguards are posted.

Local culture

  • Lunch is late and dinner later, typically from 8pm.
  • Couvert bread and olives on the table are charged; you may decline them.
  • Fado is listened to in silence — conversation during a set is genuinely rude.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Lisbon?

Three days for the central neighbourhoods, a fourth for Belém, and a fifth if you want Sintra unhurried rather than as a rushed day trip.

Is Lisbon walkable?

Yes, but it is genuinely hilly. Plan routes downhill, use the funiculars and the Santa Justa lift, and expect cobblestones throughout.

Is Sintra worth a day trip from Lisbon?

Yes, if you start early and pre-book palace tickets. By 11am the queues are long and the hill roads congested.
