Lauterbrunnen Valley

A glacial trough with seventy-two waterfalls and car-free villages hung on the cliffs above it.

Why visit Lauterbrunnen Valley

The Lauterbrunnen valley is the most vertical landscape in Europe that you can reach by train. Walls rise almost a kilometre on both sides, waterfalls drop straight off the lip, and the villages above — Mürren and Wengen — have no road access at all. It is expensive, and it is also one of the few famous landscapes that exceeds its photographs.

Best time to visit

  • Late June to September for high trails clear of snow.
  • December to March for skiing and a completely different, quieter valley.
  • May and early June: waterfalls at maximum flow from snowmelt, some high trails still closed.
  • October is beautiful, cold and increasingly shuttered.

How to get there

  • Fly to Zurich (ZRH) or Geneva (GVA), then 2.5–3 hours by train via Interlaken Ost.
  • The Swiss rail network reaches the valley floor directly; cable cars and mountain railways handle the rest.
  • Do not plan on driving to Mürren or Wengen — both are car-free.

How many days do you need

3–4 days minimum; a week if you are walking seriously.

Where to stay

Lauterbrunnen village for convenience and price, Mürren for the quietest nights and best views, Wengen for family-friendly access to the Jungfrau railway.

Best things to do in Lauterbrunnen Valley

  • Walk the Mürren–Gimmelwald path, which is short, easy and spectacular.
  • Take the Schilthorn cable car early for the Eiger–Mönch–Jungfrau line-up.
  • Walk into the Trümmelbach falls inside the mountain.
  • Hike the Lauterbrunnen valley floor to Stechelberg — flat, and unexpectedly the best angle on the walls.
  • Ride the Jungfraujoch railway if weather is genuinely clear; skip it if not.

Food

  • Alpine cheese from the valley dairies — a real seasonal product, not a souvenir.
  • Rösti and älplermagronen after a long walk.
  • Berner platte in winter, if you have earned it.
  • Bring supplies from Interlaken; village shops are small and expensive.

Budget

Switzerland is expensive and this valley is no exception. Self-catering apartments and mountain huts are the meaningful savings; the Swiss Travel Pass can be worth it for multi-day rail.

Safety & practical information

  • Mountain weather changes quickly; check the forecast and turn back early.
  • Trails are graded — respect the T-scale rather than the photographs.
  • Cable cars close in high wind, which can strand you in a car-free village overnight.

Local culture

  • Greet people on the trail — it is expected and not optional.
  • Quiet hours are observed in villages; noise carries in the valley.
  • Punctuality applies to trains and cable cars, both to the minute.

Frequently asked questions

Should you stay in Lauterbrunnen, Mürren or Wengen?

Lauterbrunnen for cost and car access, Mürren for the quietest and most dramatic setting, Wengen for the widest choice of hotels and easy Jungfrau access. All three are connected by cable car and rail.

Is the Jungfraujoch worth the cost?

Only on a genuinely clear day. It is one of the most expensive excursions in Europe, and in cloud you see nothing. Check the summit webcam the morning you plan to go.

Can you visit Lauterbrunnen without a car?

Yes, and you should. Trains reach the valley floor and cable cars handle the villages above; a car is a liability here rather than an asset.
