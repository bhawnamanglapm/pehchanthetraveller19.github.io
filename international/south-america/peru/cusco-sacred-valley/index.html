Cusco & the Sacred Valley

The Inca capital at 3,400m, with a river valley of terraced ruins and market towns leading to Machu Picchu.

Why visit Cusco & the Sacred Valley

Cusco is layered in the most literal way: Inca stonework forms the foundations of colonial churches, and both are still in use. The Sacred Valley below is a thousand metres lower, warmer, and holds the sites most visitors rush past on the way to Machu Picchu — Ollantaytambo, Moray, Maras, and weaving communities that have kept techniques continuously alive.

Best time to visit

  • May to September is the dry season and the main window.
  • April and October are shoulder months — green, fewer people, some rain.
  • November to March is the wet season; the Inca Trail closes each February for maintenance.
  • June's Inti Raymi festival fills the city; book far ahead.

How to get there

  • Fly to Cusco (CUZ) via Lima. Consider sleeping the first night in the Sacred Valley instead, which is lower and easier on arrival.
  • Trains from Ollantaytambo serve Aguas Calientes for Machu Picchu.
  • Machu Picchu entry is by timed ticket with a fixed circuit — book months ahead in season.

How many days do you need

5–7 days including acclimatisation, the valley and Machu Picchu.

Where to stay

Sacred Valley (Urubamba, Ollantaytambo) for the first nights at lower altitude, then Cusco itself for the city.

Best things to do in Cusco & the Sacred Valley

  • Acclimatise deliberately for two days before any serious walking.
  • Visit Ollantaytambo, which is both a ruin and a living Inca town.
  • See the Moray terraces and Maras salt pans in one half-day loop.
  • Book Machu Picchu with a guide and the earliest available entry slot.
  • Spend a morning with a weaving cooperative in Chinchero.

Food

  • Ceviche, even at altitude — Peru takes it seriously everywhere.
  • Cuy, if you want the traditional Andean dish.
  • Papas: hundreds of potato varieties, many of which exist nowhere else.
  • Coca tea, which is the standard local response to altitude.

Budget

Wide range from hostels to serious luxury lodges in the valley. Machu Picchu tickets, trains and guides are significant fixed costs regardless of accommodation tier.

Safety & practical information

  • Altitude sickness is the main risk. Ascend gradually, hydrate, and do not schedule hard activity on day one.
  • Taxi only from official stands or apps in Cusco.
  • Machu Picchu circuits are one-way and enforced; you cannot backtrack.

Local culture

  • Quechua is widely spoken alongside Spanish; a few words are appreciated.
  • Photographing people in traditional dress in Cusco usually carries an expected fee.
  • Buy textiles from cooperatives rather than street resellers — the difference in what reaches the weaver is large.

Frequently asked questions

How do you avoid altitude sickness in Cusco?

Spend the first two nights lower, in the Sacred Valley at around 2,800m, rather than in Cusco at 3,400m. Hydrate, avoid alcohol on arrival, and keep the first day light.

Do you need to book Machu Picchu in advance?

Yes. Entry is by timed ticket with a fixed circuit and a daily cap. In the dry season, tickets and the better time slots sell out weeks to months ahead.

Is the Inca Trail necessary to see Machu Picchu?

No. Trains from Ollantaytambo reach Aguas Calientes in under two hours. The Inca Trail is a permitted four-day trek that sells out months in advance; alternatives like the Salkantay route are less restricted.
