Banff & Lake Louise

Canada's first national park — glacial lakes, larch valleys and a walkable mountain town at the centre of it.

Why visit Banff & Lake Louise

Banff packs an unusual amount of accessible alpine landscape into a small area: turquoise lakes fed by rock flour, hikes that start at trailheads you can reach by shuttle, and wildlife that genuinely lives there rather than being sought out. The town itself is small, walkable and well set up, which makes it one of the easiest mountain destinations in the world to travel without a car.

Best time to visit

  • July to September for high trails, warm days and open teahouses.
  • Late September for larch season — a short, spectacular two-week window.
  • December to March for skiing at three resorts on one lift ticket.
  • May and June: lakes may still be frozen; check ice-out dates before booking for the colour.

How to get there

  • Fly to Calgary (YYC), then 90 minutes west by car or shuttle bus.
  • Roam and Parks Canada shuttles connect the town with Lake Louise and Moraine Lake.
  • Private vehicles cannot access Moraine Lake — shuttle or bus only.

How many days do you need

4–5 days for Banff and Lake Louise; 7–8 to add Jasper via the Icefields Parkway.

Where to stay

Banff town for restaurants and shuttle access, Lake Louise for early trailhead starts, Canmore just outside the park for better value and a local feel.

Best things to do in Banff & Lake Louise

  • Walk the Plain of Six Glaciers teahouse trail from Lake Louise.
  • Take the shuttle to Moraine Lake for the Rockpile at first light.
  • Drive or cycle a stretch of the Icefields Parkway, which deserves a full day.
  • Soak at the Banff Upper Hot Springs after a long walk.
  • Watch for wildlife from the Bow Valley Parkway at dawn, from inside the car.

Food

  • Alberta beef, unavoidable and generally very good.
  • Bison and elk on most mountain menus.
  • Good coffee culture in both Banff and Canmore.
  • Pack lunches — trail food options are limited once you leave town.

Budget

Park accommodation is limited by legislation, which keeps prices high in summer. Canmore, outside the park boundary, is consistently better value. Park entry passes are required.

Safety & practical information

  • Bear country: carry spray, know how to use it, and make noise on trails.
  • Weather changes fast above the treeline, including summer snow.
  • Never approach or feed wildlife, and stay in the vehicle for roadside sightings.

Local culture

  • This is Treaty 7 territory; several interpretive programmes are Indigenous-led and worth seeking out.
  • Leave no trace is enforced, not merely encouraged.
  • Shuttle reservations for Moraine Lake and Lake Louise sell out weeks ahead in summer.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a car in Banff?

Not necessarily. Shuttles and the Roam bus connect the town, Lake Louise and Moraine Lake. A car helps for the Icefields Parkway and dawn wildlife drives, but parking in summer is genuinely difficult.

When are the lakes their famous turquoise colour?

Once the ice melts and glacial rock flour is suspended in the water — typically from June through September. In May, Moraine and Lake Louise may still be frozen.

How many days do you need in Banff?

Four to five for the core area at a reasonable pace. Add three more if you want to drive the Icefields Parkway to Jasper properly rather than as a single long day.
