The Cedar House

A nine-room machiya conversion in Nakagyo

Overview

A restored merchant townhouse of nine rooms arranged around two courtyard gardens, five minutes' walk from Nishiki market. The conversion keeps the original cedar structure and the tsuboniwa gardens, and adds underfloor heating, deep cypress baths and a small breakfast room that opens onto the inner court.

Location

Nakagyo ward, central Kyoto — walking distance to Nishiki market, Pontocho and the Kamo river.

Why stay here

  • The machiya form is the reason to stay in Kyoto rather than any other Japanese city, and there are very few conversions of this quality left in the centre.
  • Nine rooms means the courtyards are quiet even at full occupancy.
  • Central enough to walk home from dinner, residential enough to hear nothing at night.
  • Breakfast is a proper Kyoto obanzai set rather than a hotel buffet.

Best rooms

  • The two courtyard rooms on the ground floor, which open directly onto the garden.
  • The upstairs corner room for the street-lattice light in the morning.
  • Avoid the smallest interior room if you need natural light.

Amenities

Cypress soaking baths in six roomsCourtyard gardensObanzai breakfastBicycle hireTea serviceLuggage forwarding to your next city

Who it is best for

  • Couples
  • Second-time visitors to Kyoto
  • Travellers who want architecture rather than facilities

Who it is not for

  • Families needing connecting rooms
  • Travellers who want a pool, gym or 24-hour reception

Best time to visit

November for maple colour in the courtyards; late January for the quietest weeks of the year.

Pros and considerations

Pros

  • Genuine architectural interest
  • Exceptional central location
  • Small enough for real service

Considerations

  • Traditional houses carry sound between rooms
  • Steep original staircases
  • No lift, and limited accessibility

Nearby attractions

  • Nishiki market — 5 min walk
  • Pontocho alley — 10 min walk
  • Kamo river path — 8 min walk
  • Gion — 20 min walk
