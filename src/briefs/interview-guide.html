Pehchan the Traveller · Find your identity

Five conversations

One hour each · at least three with metro professionals, 25–35

Ask about the past, never about the future. "Would you use a service that…" produces politeness. "Tell me about the last trip you wanted to take and didn't" produces facts.

Never pitch. The moment she knows what you want to hear, the data is gone. If she asks, say you are researching how Indian women plan travel.

Talk less than a third of the time. Silence is where the useful part arrives.

Write her words, not your conclusions — including the awkward ones.

A compliment is not data. "That sounds so useful" means nothing.

The questions, in order

1

Warm up

  • When did you last travel for yourself — not work, not a wedding?
  • Who decided where and when?
  • Who did you go with?
2

The trip that did not happen

  • Tell me about a trip you wanted to take and didn't.
  • What actually stopped it? Walk me through it.
  • Say nothing. Let her fill the silence.
  • Did you tell anyone at home you were thinking about it? What did they say?

The most important question in the whole plan. Everything else is context for it.

3

Permission — without using the word

  • When you plan a trip, who do you tell, and when in the process?
  • Has anyone at home ever asked you not to go somewhere? What happened?
  • What would they want to know before they were comfortable?
  • Has anyone in your family ever needed convincing? How did you do it?
4

Alone, or with people?

  • If everything were arranged, paid for and safe — would you rather go alone, or with people?
  • Whatever she says: Why?
  • Have you travelled alone before? What was the part you did not expect?

Resolves assumption 4. Four or more choosing company means "solo" comes out of the positioning.

5

Women-only

  • Have you heard of trips that are only for women?
  • Would you go on one? What appeals, what doesn't?
  • If negative: What would have to be true for you to consider it?

Resolves assumption 5. Three or more negative or indifferent, and it stops being a pitch.

6

Planning, and what it costs her

  • Walk me through planning your last trip. What did you do first?
  • How long did that take?
  • What was the most annoying part?
  • Has anyone ever planned a trip for you? How was it?
7

Hand her the document — say nothing about it

  • What is this? What would you use it for?
  • Then: Would you show this to anyone?
  • Only if she says yes: Who, and why them?

The highest-value minute of the hour. Watch what she does with it, not what she says about it.

8

Close

  • Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
  • Who else should I talk to?

What counts as a signal

Strong — write it downWeak — politeness
Names a specific trip that didn't happen, and who stopped it"Safety is a concern for women"
Describes convincing someone, unprompted"My family is quite supportive"
Asks to keep the document"This looks really nice"
Offers to introduce someone"Good luck with it"
Asks what it costs"I would definitely use this"
Listen for the last row. Nobody asks the price of something they do not want. If she asks, the answer is ₹2,500 — say it without apologising and then stop talking.
