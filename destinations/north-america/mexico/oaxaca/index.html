Oaxaca

Mexico's culinary and craft capital — mezcal valleys, Zapotec archaeology and a walkable colonial centre.

Why visit Oaxaca

Oaxaca is the reason to travel to Mexico for something other than a beach. Sixteen Indigenous cultures, a food tradition with genuine regional depth, weaving and pottery villages within an hour's drive, and Monte Albán on the ridge above the city. It is also one of the best-value premium destinations in the Americas.

Best time to visit

  • October to April is dry and warm — the reliable window.
  • Late October for Día de Muertos, which is extraordinary and requires booking months ahead.
  • July for the Guelaguetza dance festival.
  • May to September brings afternoon rain but the valleys are green and prices drop.

How to get there

  • Fly into Oaxaca (OAX) via Mexico City, or drive the toll road in around six hours.
  • The historic centre is compact and walkable.
  • Hire a driver or join a collective for the valley villages and mezcal palenques.

How many days do you need

4–5 days for city and valleys; 8–10 to add the Pacific coast.

Where to stay

The Centro Histórico for walkability, Jalatlaco or Xochimilco for quieter neighbourhood stays, or a valley hacienda for space and views.

Best things to do in Oaxaca

  • Eat through the Mercado 20 de Noviembre, slowly.
  • Visit Monte Albán early, before the heat and the buses.
  • Spend a day in the craft villages — Teotitlán for weaving, San Bartolo for black pottery.
  • Tour a family palenque and understand mezcal as agriculture rather than a bar drink.
  • Take a cooking class that starts at the market.

Food

  • The seven moles, of which mole negro is the most famous and the most work.
  • Tlayudas, best from a street grill at night.
  • Chapulines, if you are willing.
  • Mezcal, tasted at room temperature and sipped, never shot.

Budget

Very good value. Boutique hotels in restored colonial houses cost a fraction of comparable properties in Europe, and eating well is inexpensive at every tier.

Safety & practical information

  • Oaxaca is among the safer states for travellers; normal city precautions apply.
  • Altitude is around 1,550m — mild but noticeable on arrival.
  • Tap water is not drinkable; bottled or filtered water is standard everywhere.

Local culture

  • Indigenous communities have their own governance; ask before photographing in villages.
  • Craft prices are set by makers, and hard bargaining on artisan work is poor form.
  • Día de Muertos is a family remembrance, not a costume party — attend cemeteries respectfully or not at all.

Frequently asked questions

Is Oaxaca worth visiting for food alone?

Many people go for exactly that. The regional cooking has more depth than any other part of Mexico, and market eating, cooking classes and mezcal palenque visits can easily fill four days.

When is Día de Muertos in Oaxaca?

Concentrated around 31 October to 2 November. Accommodation books out several months in advance and prices rise sharply, so plan early or choose different dates.

Is Oaxaca safe for tourists?

Oaxaca state consistently reports lower crime levels than much of Mexico, and the tourist centre is well policed. Standard city awareness is sufficient; check your government's current travel advice before booking.
