Amalfi Coast

Vertical villages above the Tyrrhenian — lemon terraces, ferry hops and a coast road that is best not driven.

Why visit Amalfi Coast

The Amalfi Coast earns its reputation and its crowds. Thirteen towns stack up a limestone wall above the sea, each with a different character: Ravello is high, quiet and musical; Positano is photogenic and busy; Atrani, next door to Amalfi, is where people actually live. Travelled by ferry and on the old mule paths, it is a completely different trip from the one experienced through a coach window.

Best time to visit

  • May, early June, and late September are the best weeks: warm sea, open businesses, manageable crowds.
  • July and August are hot, crowded and expensive.
  • Late October through March, much of the coast closes; Ravello and Amalfi stay open.
  • The Path of the Gods walk is best in spring, before the heat.

How to get there

  • Fly to Naples (NAP), then ferry from Salerno or Sorrento in season — far better than the coast road.
  • By car, expect slow going and near-impossible parking. Most stays charge separately for it.
  • SITA buses run the coast road year-round and are cheap, crowded and scenic.

How many days do you need

4–5 days, or 7 combined with Naples or Capri.

Where to stay

Ravello for quiet and views, Praiano for balance and value, Positano for the postcard at a premium, Atrani for a village that still feels like one.

Best things to do in Amalfi Coast

  • Walk the Sentiero degli Dei from Bomerano down to Nocelle.
  • Take the ferry rather than the bus at least once — the coast reads differently from the water.
  • Visit Villa Cimbrone's belvedere in Ravello early in the day.
  • Spend a morning on a lemon terrace with a family that still farms it.
  • Boat out to the Li Galli islands in the late afternoon light.

Food

  • Scialatielli ai frutti di mare, the local hand-cut pasta.
  • Delizia al limone — the lemon dessert that belongs to this coast.
  • Mozzarella di bufala from the Sele plain, an hour away.
  • Anchovies from Cetara, cured and pressed into colatura.

Budget

Expensive in season, particularly for sea-view rooms. Praiano and the inland villages are meaningfully better value than Positano for the same coastline.

Safety & practical information

  • Very safe; the real hazards are the coast road, steep steps and strong sun.
  • Many towns are stair-heavy and difficult with limited mobility or large luggage.
  • Ferries cancel in rough weather — build a buffer before onward flights.

Local culture

  • Lunch and dinner run late; kitchens often close between services.
  • Cover up when entering churches, including the Amalfi duomo.
  • Book restaurants in season — walk-ins are optimistic in July and August.

Frequently asked questions

Should you rent a car on the Amalfi Coast?

Usually no. The coast road is narrow and slow in season, parking is scarce and expensive, and ferries plus buses reach everywhere you are likely to go.

Which Amalfi Coast town should you stay in?

Ravello for calm and views, Praiano for balance and value, Positano for the classic setting at the highest price, Atrani for a lived-in village feel.

When is the Amalfi Coast least crowded?

Late April to mid-May, and the last two weeks of September into early October — warm enough to swim, without August's density.
