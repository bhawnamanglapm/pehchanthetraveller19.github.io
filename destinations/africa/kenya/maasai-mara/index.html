Maasai Mara

Open savanna with the highest predator density in Africa, and the northern half of the great wildebeest migration.

Why visit Maasai Mara

The Mara is the classic East African savanna: rolling grassland, riverine forest and an ecosystem continuous with the Serengeti across the Tanzanian border. Predator sightings are more reliable here than almost anywhere, and the community conservancies bordering the national reserve allow off-road driving, night drives and walking safaris that the reserve itself does not.

Best time to visit

  • July to October for the migration river crossings — the busiest and most expensive months.
  • January to March: green, quiet, excellent for big cats and calving.
  • April to May is the long rains — lowest prices, some camps close, roads difficult.
  • Wildlife is resident year-round; the migration is a bonus rather than the whole story.

How to get there

  • Fly Nairobi Wilson to one of the Mara airstrips in about 45 minutes — much better than the 5–6 hour road transfer.
  • International arrivals come through Nairobi (NBO).
  • Game drives are in camp vehicles with guides; self-driving is possible in the reserve but not recommended.

How many days do you need

3–4 nights. Two is too short to absorb a transfer day; five allows a conservancy and reserve combination.

Where to stay

Conservancy camps for exclusivity, low vehicle density and walking or night drives; reserve camps for lower cost and proximity to the Mara river crossings.

Best things to do in Maasai Mara

  • Take a dawn game drive — the first two hours are the best of the day.
  • Spend time with a Maasai guide from the community that owns the land.
  • Book a walking safari in a conservancy where it is permitted.
  • Consider a balloon flight at sunrise if the budget allows.
  • Sit still at a single sighting for an hour instead of chasing the radio.

Food

  • Camp catering is generally excellent and included in rates.
  • Nyama choma, Kenya's grilled meat, on the way through Nairobi.
  • Kenyan coffee and tea, both grown a few hours away.
  • Sundowners on a ridge — a genuine safari ritual rather than a marketing one.

Budget

Safari pricing is all-inclusive and structured by season. Conservancy camps carry a premium for exclusivity and the activities they permit; peak migration months are the highest of the year.

Safety & practical information

  • Follow guide instructions absolutely — this is wild, unfenced country.
  • Malaria prophylaxis is advised; consult a travel clinic well in advance.
  • Do not walk outside camp at night without an escort, ever.

Local culture

  • Ask before photographing Maasai people; a fee or a formal visit arrangement is normal.
  • Conservancy models pay landowning communities directly — choosing them is an ethical decision as well as an experiential one.
  • Tipping guides and camp staff is customary and meaningful.

Frequently asked questions

When is the wildebeest migration in the Maasai Mara?

The herds are typically in the Mara from July to October, with river crossings concentrated in that window. Exact timing depends on rainfall and cannot be predicted precisely in advance.

Conservancy or national reserve?

Conservancies limit vehicle numbers and permit off-road, night and walking activities, at a higher price. The reserve is cheaper and holds the famous river crossings, but is busier at sightings.

How many nights do you need on safari in the Mara?

Three to four nights. With flying transfers, that gives you three full days of drives — enough to see the ecosystem rather than a highlight reel.
