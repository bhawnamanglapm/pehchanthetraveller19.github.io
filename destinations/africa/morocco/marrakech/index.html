Marrakech

A walled medina of workshops and riads with the High Atlas an hour away and the Sahara beyond it.

Why visit Marrakech

Marrakech is a craft city first and a tourist city second, and the difference shows if you get past the main square. The medina's souks are still organised by trade — dyers, metalworkers, leather, wood — and the riads behind their blank walls are among the most rewarding accommodation anywhere. The Atlas mountains an hour south make it one of the few cities where you can have breakfast in a courtyard and lunch at two thousand metres.

Best time to visit

  • March to May and October to November: warm days, cool evenings.
  • December to February is cool and bright, with snow on the Atlas as a backdrop.
  • June to August is very hot — riads with pools become essential rather than nice.
  • Ramadan changes the rhythm of the city; visiting is rewarding but plan around it.

How to get there

  • Marrakech Menara (RAK) is fifteen minutes from the medina.
  • Direct trains connect Casablanca, Rabat and Tangier.
  • Walk in the medina — cars cannot reach most riads. Petit taxis handle the new town.

How many days do you need

3–4 days for the city, 6–7 with the Atlas or a desert extension.

Where to stay

A medina riad for the authentic experience, Hivernage or Gueliz for hotel conveniences and pools, or the Palmeraie for resort quiet outside the city.

Best things to do in Marrakech

  • Get lost in the souks deliberately, in the morning when they are working rather than selling.
  • Visit the Ben Youssef Madrasa and the Saadian tombs early.
  • Spend a half day at a hammam — a real neighbourhood one or a spa one, both worthwhile.
  • Drive to the Ourika or Imlil valleys in the Atlas for the day.
  • Take a rooftop seat at Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk and simply watch.

Food

  • Tagine, though the good ones are slow and rarely in the tourist square.
  • Mechoui — slow-roasted lamb, traditionally a morning food.
  • Tanjia, the Marrakchi men's dish cooked in the hammam furnace.
  • Mint tea, poured from height, throughout the day.

Budget

Excellent value across tiers. Riads range from simple family houses to serious luxury; the Atlas and Agafay have a growing high-end camp market.

Safety & practical information

  • Generally safe, with persistent commercial pressure rather than danger.
  • Unofficial guides and 'the souk is closed today' redirection are common — decline politely and keep walking.
  • Agree taxi fares before getting in, or insist on the meter.

Local culture

  • Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered — particularly in the medina.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially in the square, where a fee is expected.
  • Friday is the main prayer day; the city is quieter and some businesses close.

Frequently asked questions

Should you stay in a riad or a hotel in Marrakech?

A riad, for at least part of the stay. They are the local architectural form — courtyard houses turned inward — and staying in one is a large part of the experience. Hotels outside the walls offer pools and easier arrival.

Is Marrakech safe for solo female travellers?

Many travel here alone without issue, but persistent attention and commercial pressure are common. Dressing modestly, walking with purpose and booking a riad that arranges medina pickup all help significantly.

Can you visit the Sahara from Marrakech?

Yes, but it is a serious drive — around ten hours each way to Merzouga. Do it over three days minimum, or choose the Agafay stone desert an hour away for a shorter alternative.
