AlUla

Nabataean tombs cut into sandstone outcrops across a desert oasis valley that has only recently reopened to travellers.

Why visit AlUla

AlUla holds Hegra — the Nabataean city that shares its builders with Petra, and holds a fraction of its visitors. Around it sits a valley of eroded sandstone, a date-palm oasis, an abandoned mud-brick old town and a growing set of serious design hotels. It is one of the few genuinely significant archaeological landscapes in the world where you can still stand alone in front of a two-thousand-year-old façade.

Best time to visit

  • November to March is the practical window; days are mild and nights cold.
  • December to February is peak, with the largest cultural programme.
  • Avoid May to September — daytime heat is extreme and outdoor sites become unsafe.
  • October and April are shoulder months and can still be very hot.

How to get there

  • AlUla International (ULH) has seasonal direct flights; otherwise connect via Riyadh or Jeddah.
  • Driving from Medina takes around three and a half hours on good roads.
  • Hire a car or use the local shuttles; sites are spread across a wide valley.

How many days do you need

3–4 days for the main sites without rushing.

Where to stay

The Ashar and Ashar-adjacent valleys for luxury desert lodges, or AlUla old town area for smaller, simpler and more central stays.

Best things to do in AlUla

  • Visit Hegra with a licensed rawi guide — access is only by official tour.
  • Walk the AlUla old town and its oasis path in the late afternoon.
  • See Elephant Rock at dusk rather than midday.
  • Book stargazing in the Gharameel field, where the darkness is genuinely deep.
  • Cycle the oasis trail between date farms in the early morning.

Food

  • Dates from the oasis, in more varieties than you knew existed.
  • Saleeg, the creamy Hijazi rice dish.
  • Mandi and madfoon slow-cooked lamb.
  • Arabic coffee with cardamom, served as a matter of hospitality rather than commerce.

Budget

The stay market skews high — this is a deliberately positioned luxury destination. Site tickets and guided tours are additional and should be booked ahead.

Safety & practical information

  • Very low crime; the real risks are heat, dehydration and driving distances.
  • Carry far more water than feels necessary for any outdoor site.
  • Site access is regulated — independent wandering at Hegra is not permitted.

Local culture

  • Dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered for all visitors.
  • Alcohol is prohibited nationwide.
  • Prayer times briefly close some shops and services; plan around them.

Getting oriented

Use the map to place AlUla in its region before you plan transfers — distances in guides are frequently longer in practice than they look on a page.

Frequently asked questions

Is AlUla the same as Petra?

No, but they share the Nabataean civilisation. Hegra was the southern city of the same kingdom. It is smaller than Petra and far less visited, which is much of its appeal.

When should you visit AlUla?

November through March. Outside that window, daytime temperatures make the outdoor archaeological sites genuinely unsafe for extended visits.

Can you visit Hegra independently?

No. Access is by official guided tour only, booked in advance through the AlUla visitor system. This is one reason the site remains in such good condition.
