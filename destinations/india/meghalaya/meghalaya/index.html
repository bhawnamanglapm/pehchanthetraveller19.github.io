Meghalaya

The wettest inhabited place on earth — living root bridges, sandstone gorges and Khasi villages in permanent green.

Why visit Meghalaya

Meghalaya is India's least-travelled beautiful state. The Khasi hills hold living root bridges grown over generations from rubber fig roots, canyons that drop into Bangladesh, caves among the longest in Asia, and villages that have made cleanliness and forest stewardship a matter of collective pride. It rains harder here than almost anywhere on the planet, and everything about the landscape follows from that.

Best time to visit

  • October to April is dry and clear — the main season.
  • November to February is coolest and best for trekking.
  • May to September is the monsoon, when Cherrapunji and Mawsynram record extraordinary rainfall.
  • September and early October offer full waterfalls with less rain.

How to get there

  • Fly to Guwahati (GAU) in Assam, then three hours by road to Shillong.
  • Shillong Airport has limited connections; most travellers use Guwahati.
  • Hire a car with a driver — distances are short but roads are slow and winding.

How many days do you need

6–7 days for the Khasi and Jaintia hills at a reasonable pace.

Where to stay

Shillong for the base, Cherrapunji for the gorges and falls, and homestays in Nongriat, Mawlynnong or Kongthong for the villages.

Best things to do in Meghalaya

  • Walk down to the double-decker living root bridge at Nongriat and stay the night rather than rushing back up.
  • Kayak or swim in the Umngot river at Dawki when the water runs clear.
  • Visit Mawlynnong and Kongthong, the whistling village where people are called by melody.
  • Explore Mawsmai or Arwah caves.
  • Take the Laitlum canyon walk at sunrise.

Food

  • Jadoh, the Khasi rice and pork dish.
  • Smoked pork with bamboo shoot.
  • Tungrymbai, fermented soybean — pungent and excellent.
  • Local rice beer, offered in villages as hospitality.

Budget

Inexpensive. Homestays and small guesthouses dominate; there is limited luxury inventory, which is part of the appeal.

Safety & practical information

  • Very safe, with strong community governance in Khasi villages.
  • The Nongriat descent and ascent is 3,000+ steps each way — genuinely strenuous.
  • Leeches are common in the wet months; roads can wash out in monsoon.

Local culture

  • Khasi society is matrilineal — property passes through the youngest daughter.
  • Villages are community-managed; pay the local entry fees, they fund maintenance.
  • Ask before photographing in villages, and follow the plastic bans strictly.

Frequently asked questions

How difficult is the trek to the double-decker root bridge?

Around 3,000 steps down to Nongriat and the same back up, on stone stairs that are slippery when wet. Most fit walkers manage it in two to three hours each way. Staying overnight in Nongriat makes it far more enjoyable.

When should you visit Meghalaya?

October to April for dry weather and clear river water at Dawki. September gives you full waterfalls with less rain, which many travellers consider the best compromise.

Is Meghalaya safe for solo travellers?

Yes. Khasi villages have strong community governance and the state sees very little crime against visitors. The practical risks are road conditions, monsoon weather and strenuous trails rather than personal safety.
