Spiti Valley

A cold desert at 4,000m between the Himalaya and the Tibetan plateau — monasteries, moonscape and very few people.

Why visit Spiti Valley

Spiti is one of the least populated places in India and one of the most striking. The valley floor sits above 3,500m in a rain shadow that produces bare ochre mountains, glacial rivers and thousand-year-old Buddhist monasteries at Tabo and Key. It is a hard place to reach and a harder place to rush, and it rewards travellers who commit real time to it.

Best time to visit

  • June to September is the main season, when both approach roads are open.
  • July and August bring landslide risk on the Manali side but the fullest access.
  • October is clear, cold and beautiful, with services winding down.
  • November to May: the Manali road is closed by snow; Shimla–Kinnaur access is possible but severe.

How to get there

  • Two road routes: Shimla–Kinnaur (gradual, better for acclimatisation) and Manali–Kunzum (shorter, higher, seasonal).
  • Nearest airports are Bhuntar (KUU) and Chandigarh (IXC), both followed by long drives.
  • Enter via Shimla and exit via Manali to gain altitude gradually — this is the single most important planning decision here.

How many days do you need

8–10 days minimum for the loop with proper acclimatisation.

Where to stay

Homestays in Kaza, Langza, Komic and Demul — the village homestay network here is community-run and the best way to stay.

Best things to do in Spiti Valley

  • Stay at least two nights in Kaza before going higher.
  • Visit Key monastery in the early morning during prayers.
  • Walk to Dhankar lake above the old monastery.
  • Stay in a village homestay at Langza or Komic, among the highest inhabited villages in the world.
  • Drive to Chandratal lake if the road is open — camp only at the designated site.

Food

  • Thukpa and thenthuk, the Tibetan noodle soups that make sense at altitude.
  • Momos, everywhere and always.
  • Seabuckthorn juice, grown locally and full of vitamin C.
  • Butter tea, which is an acquired taste and an effective one.

Budget

Inexpensive by design — homestays and simple guesthouses dominate, and there is very little luxury inventory. Fuel and vehicle hire are the main costs.

Safety & practical information

  • Altitude is the primary risk. Ascend via Shimla, sleep low, and know the symptoms of AMS.
  • Mobile coverage is limited to BSNL in most of the valley; carry cash.
  • Roads are subject to landslides and river crossings; never travel a closed route.

Local culture

  • Spiti is culturally Tibetan Buddhist — circumambulate stupas clockwise.
  • Ask before entering monastery prayer halls and never photograph during prayers without permission.
  • Water is scarce; use it as villagers do, not as a hotel guest would.

Frequently asked questions

How do you acclimatise for Spiti Valley?

Enter via Shimla and Kinnaur, which climbs gradually over several days, rather than crossing directly from Manali over the 4,550m Kunzum pass. Sleep at progressively higher elevations and build rest days into the plan.

When is Spiti Valley open?

The Manali route typically opens from June to October depending on snow clearance. The Shimla–Kinnaur route stays open most of the year but is severe in winter. Always check current road status before travelling.

Is Spiti suitable for a first Himalayan trip?

It is demanding — altitude, long drives, basic facilities, limited medical access. Travellers with previous high-altitude experience will find it far more comfortable, though a well-paced ten-day trip with a good driver is achievable for most healthy people.
