Munnar

Tea country at 1,600m in the Western Ghats — contoured hillsides, shola forest and cool mornings above the Kerala heat.

Why visit Munnar

Munnar is what the Western Ghats look like when you climb out of the coastal humidity: rolling tea estates planted more than a century ago, cloud forest in the folds between them, and the Eravikulam plateau above holding the Nilgiri tahr. The town itself is unremarkable; the estates, the high ranges and the small roads between them are the reason to come.

Best time to visit

  • September to March: clear, cool and the reliable window.
  • December to February is coldest and driest, with the best visibility.
  • April and May are warm but bearable, and much quieter.
  • June to August is heavy monsoon — dramatic, green, and landslide-prone.

How to get there

  • Fly to Kochi (COK), then a four-hour drive up through the ghats.
  • Madurai (IXM) is a similar distance from the Tamil Nadu side.
  • Hire a car with a driver — the ghat roads are winding and public transport is slow.

How many days do you need

3 days in Munnar; 8–10 within a wider Kerala trip with the backwaters and coast.

Where to stay

Estate bungalows and hillside resorts outside town for the views and quiet; avoid Munnar town centre itself, which is congested.

Best things to do in Munnar

  • Walk a tea estate at dawn with a plantation guide.
  • Visit Eravikulam National Park early for the tahr and the plateau.
  • Drive up to Top Station for the view into Tamil Nadu.
  • Take the Kolukkumalai jeep track to the highest tea estate in the world.
  • Spend an afternoon doing nothing at all on an estate verandah — this is the point of Munnar.

Food

  • Kerala breakfast: appam with stew, puttu with kadala curry.
  • Estate-grown tea, tasted properly at a plantation.
  • Beef fry, which in Kerala is a genuine regional speciality.
  • Fresh cardamom and pepper from the surrounding spice hills.

Budget

Wide range, from simple homestays to restored colonial estate bungalows. Kerala's premium hill properties are excellent value compared with international equivalents.

Safety & practical information

  • Very safe, including for solo travellers.
  • Monsoon landslides can close ghat roads with little warning.
  • Leeches on forest walks during and after the rains — socks and salt help.

Local culture

  • Kerala has India's highest literacy rate and a strong political culture; conversations get interesting quickly.
  • Modest dress is appreciated in villages and temples.
  • Many estates are working plantations — ask before walking into a section.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Munnar?

Three. One for Eravikulam and the high ranges, one for the estates and Kolukkumalai, and one deliberately unscheduled — the weather here rewards flexibility.

Is Munnar worth visiting in monsoon?

It is at its most beautiful and its most difficult. Landslides close roads, views disappear into cloud for days, and prices are at their lowest. Go if you want green and solitude, not if you want a fixed itinerary.

What is the best way to combine Munnar with the rest of Kerala?

The classic route is Kochi, Munnar, Thekkady, Alleppey backwaters, then a coastal beach at Marari or Varkala — around ten days at a comfortable pace.
