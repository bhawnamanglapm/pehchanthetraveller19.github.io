Indonesia · Bali

Ubud

Bali's cultural interior — rice terraces, river gorges and a stay market that runs from simple homestays to some of Asia's best-designed hotels.

Why visit Ubud

Ubud is where Bali is a working culture rather than a beach. Ceremonies happen on a calendar that has nothing to do with tourism, the gorges hold genuinely beautiful architecture, and the subak irrigation system that shapes the rice terraces is a thousand-year-old piece of cooperative engineering. The centre is busy; the villages fifteen minutes out are not.

Best time to visit

  • April to October is the dry season and the reliable window.
  • May, June and September are the sweet spot: dry, green, and either side of the peak.
  • November to March brings afternoon rain — often brief, sometimes not.
  • July and August are peak; book stays and drivers well ahead.

How to get there

  • Fly into Denpasar (DPS); Ubud is 60–90 minutes north by car, longer in traffic.
  • Pre-arranged transfers are simpler than airport negotiation.
  • Hire a driver by the day for temple and terrace circuits; distances are short but roads are slow.

How many days do you need

4–5 days in Ubud, ideally within a 10–14 day Bali trip that includes the coast.

Where to stay

Central Ubud for walkability, the Sayan and Payangan ridges for river-gorge views and calm, or Sidemen further east if you want rice country without the crowds.

Best things to do in Ubud

  • Walk the Campuhan ridge at first light.
  • Watch a legong or kecak performance staged for the community, not only for visitors.
  • Cycle down through the Tegallalang villages rather than stopping at the terrace viewpoint.
  • Book a proper Balinese healing or offering-making session with a family, arranged respectfully.
  • Take the pre-dawn drive to Mount Batur for the caldera sunrise if you are fit for it.

Food

  • Babi guling — the celebratory suckling pig, best from a warung at lunch.
  • Bebek betutu, slow-cooked duck in banana leaf, usually ordered a day ahead.
  • Nasi campur as a daily default — every warung's version differs.
  • A genuinely strong plant-based scene, which is unusual for the region.

Budget

Ubud has the widest stay range of anywhere in this guide — simple family homestays through to internationally significant design hotels in the gorges.

Safety & practical information

  • Generally safe; the main issues are traffic, scooter injuries and stomach upsets.
  • Monkeys at the Sacred Forest will take anything loose — leave bags at the hotel.
  • Respect ceremony road closures; they take priority over your schedule.

Local culture

  • Sarong and sash are required at temples and usually provided.
  • Do not step over the daily canang sari offerings on the pavement.
  • Ask before photographing people at ceremonies, and never at funerals unless invited.

Getting oriented

Use the map to place Ubud in its region before you plan transfers — distances in guides are frequently longer in practice than they look on a page.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ubud too crowded now?

The central streets and the famous terrace viewpoints are genuinely busy. Fifteen minutes out — Sayan, Keliki, Sidemen — the density drops sharply, and mornings before eight are quiet almost everywhere.

How long should you stay in Ubud?

Four to five days. Any less and you spend the trip in transit; any more and most travellers want to move to the coast.

Do you need a driver in Ubud?

For temple circuits and day trips, yes — roads are slow and parking is difficult. Within the town centre, walk.
