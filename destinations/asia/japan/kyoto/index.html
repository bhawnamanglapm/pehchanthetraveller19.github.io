Kyoto

Eleven centuries of imperial craft in a valley of cedar hills — best travelled early in the morning and on foot.

Why visit Kyoto

Kyoto is not a city you tick off; it is a city you learn to read. The temples that appear on every list are worth seeing, but the real texture is in the machiya townhouses of Nishijin, the tofu shops that have been in the same family for six generations, and the way the hills change colour twice a year with a precision that the whole country plans holidays around. Come for the temples, stay for the craft districts.

Best time to visit

  • Late March to mid-April for cherry blossom — beautiful, and the busiest weeks of the year.
  • November for maple colour, which is arguably better and equally crowded.
  • January and February are cold, quiet and the most atmospheric time to have a temple garden to yourself.
  • Avoid mid-July to August unless you tolerate humidity well.

How to get there

  • Fly into Kansai International (KIX) or Osaka Itami, then 75–90 minutes by train or airport limousine bus.
  • From Tokyo, the Tokaido Shinkansen takes a little over two hours and is usually better than flying door-to-door.
  • Kyoto is a walking and bus city; a bicycle is the single best travel decision you can make here.

How many days do you need

4–5 days for a first visit; 7 if you want day trips to Nara and the Kii coast.

Where to stay

Base yourself in Gion or Higashiyama for evening atmosphere, Nakagyo for food and walkability, or Arashiyama if you want quiet mornings and are happy commuting in.

Best things to do in Kyoto

  • Walk the Philosopher's Path at 7am, before the tour groups arrive.
  • Spend an afternoon in the Nishijin weaving district rather than another temple.
  • Take the Kurama-to-Kibune hill walk for cedar forest and an onsen at the end.
  • Book a tea ceremony that teaches the practice rather than performs it.
  • Ride out to Fushimi for sake breweries, which most visitors skip entirely.

Food

  • Kaiseki — the multi-course seasonal meal Kyoto invented. Book weeks ahead.
  • Obanzai — the everyday home-style counterpart, far easier to find and often more memorable.
  • Yudofu tofu hot pot in Nanzen-ji, especially in winter.
  • Nishiki Market for produce, though eat around it rather than in it.

Budget

Kyoto spans the full range. Guesthouses and standing-counter meals make it genuinely affordable; ryokan with private onsen and kaiseki dinner sit at the top of the luxury market.

Safety & practical information

  • Among the safest cities in the world for solo and female travellers.
  • Cash is still useful at smaller temples, markets and older restaurants.
  • Photography restrictions in Gion's private lanes are enforced — respect the signage.

Local culture

  • Punctuality is a courtesy, not a formality — arrive on time for reservations.
  • Remove shoes where you see a step up and a shoe rack.
  • Quiet on public transport is the norm; phone calls are not.

Getting oriented

Use the map to place Kyoto in its region before you plan transfers — distances in guides are frequently longer in practice than they look on a page.

Map

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Kyoto?

Four to five days lets you see the major temple clusters without rushing, plus one district day and one day trip. Two days is enough only if you accept a highlights loop.

What is the best time to visit Kyoto?

Late March to mid-April for blossom and November for autumn colour are the most spectacular and the most crowded. For a calmer trip with excellent light, come in late January or early February.

Is Kyoto walkable?

Largely yes, within districts. Between districts, use the bus and subway, or hire a bicycle — Kyoto is flat and cycling is the fastest way across the city.
