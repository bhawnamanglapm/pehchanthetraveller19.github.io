# Stage 2 — Market

Researched 29 Sep 2026. `docs/15` called this "the most important gap in this
document": competitive work had been done on pilgrimage and adventure, then the
product became women's travel and no research followed it.

This closes it. The conclusion is not the one the product was heading towards.

---

## The category is twenty years old and has never taken a rupee of outside money

| | Since | Scale | Funding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WOW Club** (Sumitra Senapaty) | **2005** | **~₹18 crore revenue**. 10,000+ women over 17 years, now **3,000–4,000 a year** across **125 trips**, with **12 employees** | Bootstrapped |
| **Girls On The Go** (Piya Bose) | 2008 | — | Bootstrapped |
| **F5 Escapes** (Malini Gowrishankar) | 2013 | Groups of 8–10 | Bootstrapped |
| **Jugni** | 2014/15 | 5,000+ women, 55+ countries | Bootstrapped |
| **The Flapper Life** (Zinal Doshi, ex-banker) | 2016 | Mumbai, all-female team | Bootstrapped |
| **Women on Clouds** (Shireen Mehra, ex-cabin crew) | — | Trips plus a paid members' club | Bootstrapped |
| **Wandering Jane** (Garima Pande, Akshat Sharma) | — | 20% month-on-month growth | Bootstrapped |

**Not one of them has raised external funding in twenty years.** That fact
carries more information than any market-size estimate, and it can be read two
ways: either the category is not venture-scale, or it is overlooked. WOW Club's
numbers settle it.

## The unit economics, which are public and which are thin

Two figures do most of the work here.

**Wandering Jane works on a 10% margin** — roughly 40% of the package price goes
to accommodation and 50% to activities and services. That is the shape of tour
operating everywhere: you are a pass-through for other people's inventory.

**WOW Club turns over ~₹18 crore on 3,000–4,000 travellers a year with 12
staff.** That is about **₹51,000 of revenue per traveller**, which matches the
sticker price of a single trip — so the revenue *is* gross bookings, not a take
rate. At a 10% operator margin that is on the order of ₹1.8 crore of gross
margin across twelve people, after twenty years and 125 trips a year.

That is a real, respectable, durable business. **It is not a platform, and no
amount of software makes it one.** Tour operating makes money on volume, and
volume in this category took two decades to reach four thousand women a year.

## The scale end is already taken, by people who are better at it

The competitive set is not five boutique operators. It has three tiers:

| Tier | Who | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Mainstream at scale** | **Veena World** (56 women's packages), **Kesari** ("My Fair Lady") | Groups of 30–50. National distribution, decades of supplier relationships |
| **Established specialist** | WOW Club | 125 trips a year, ₹18 Cr |
| **Boutique** | F5, Jugni, Flapper Life, Women on Clouds, Wandering Jane, Girls On The Go | Groups of 8–12, founder-led, bootstrapped |

Entering as tour operator number nine means competing on trips against people
with twenty years of supplier relationships, at a 10% margin, with no capital
and no distribution. Wandering Jane says the quiet part out loud: bootstrapped,
growing 20% month on month, and **cannot afford to market itself**.

## The one thing the whole category charges for, and nobody defends

**F5 Escapes, Ladakh, 7 nights: ₹58,000 + 5% GST. Single occupancy surcharge:
₹19,750 + 5% GST.**

A woman travelling alone pays **34% more** — or she shares a room with a
stranger. Jugni is twin-share by default; single rooms cost extra. This is the
category norm.

Set that against our own survey. Asked whether they would share a room with
another woman to reduce cost, respondents said *yes*, *yes*, **"maybe,
depending on the person/property"**, **"maybe, depending on the person/property"**.
Half would not commit.

So the industry's answer to a woman travelling alone is: *share with a stranger,
or pay a third more.* Notably, **Veena World and Kesari — the big mainstream
operators — already solve this**, guaranteeing a roommate with no single
occupancy charge. The boutique women-only operators, the ones whose entire
pitch is serving solo women, are the ones charging the penalty.

That is a genuine gap. It is also, precisely, a tour-operator's problem to
solve, and solving it requires inventory, volume and capital — the three things
this business does not have.

## What this means, stated plainly

**Do not become the ninth women-only tour operator.**

The evidence against it is now specific rather than cautionary:

1. Twenty years, zero outside funding, 10% margins, and the best-performing
   operator reaches 4,000 women a year with 12 staff
2. Veena World and Kesari own the scale end and already beat the specialists on
   the single-supplement problem
3. `docs/15` found that ~80% of travel startups fail, with "no differentiation"
   the leading cause — and a ninth women-only operator is the definition of it
4. Our own survey rates women-only groups **3.7/5**, female trip leaders
   **3.7**, female drivers **2.7** — the lowest-scoring ideas in it
5. Nobody in the survey would book a women-only group trip

**The document is a different business.** It has no cost of goods, no
inventory, no supplier relationships to build, and no volume threshold. It is
the one thing none of these nine companies sells, and it is the only thing this
project has evidence for: written, shown to a family, and it worked.

Put the two side by side:

| | Tour operator | The document |
|---|---|---|
| Margin | ~10%, pass-through | No cost of goods |
| Needs | Inventory, volume, capital, supplier relationships | A founder who has travelled |
| Competitors | Nine, one with twenty years' head start, two at national scale | **None found** |
| Evidence it works | Plenty — for them | One observed case — for us |
| Ceiling | ₹18 Cr after twenty years | Unknown, untested |

The honest reading is that the second column is small and unproven, and the
first is proven and thin. This is not a claim that the document business is
large. It is a claim that **the operator business is a bad trade at this size,
and the document is the only defensible position available.**

## The relationship worth having with them

These nine are better read as **distribution than as rivals**. Every one of
them has the same problem: a woman wants to come, and her family will not agree.
They sell the trip; they do not sell the permission. A document that gets a
family to yes converts *their* hesitant enquiries too.

That is speculative and should be marked as such. But it is the first framing
in which their twenty-year head start is an asset rather than a wall.

## 2026 update — the incumbent moved in

Added 29 Sep after a pass for current-year data. The research above leaned on
2022–2025 figures. Three 2026 developments change things, and one of them
matters a great deal.

### MakeMyTrip has launched women's safety features at national scale

This is the single most important competitive fact in this document, and the
first pass missed it.

MakeMyTrip has introduced women-centric safety and assurance signals across
stays and intercity buses:

- **Reviews written by women highlighted**, with AI summaries pulling out staff
  behaviour, neighbourhood safety and comfort
- **Women-specific amenity indicators** surfaced on listings — CCTV, door eye,
  door chains, full-length mirrors
- **On buses, booking one side of a double berth automatically restricts the
  adjacent berth to female-only**
- Built on **~97,000 accommodation properties and 3,500+ intercity bus
  operators** of structured partner data, plus millions of reviews

Separately, MakeMyTrip is working with OpenAI on AI-led travel discovery.

**Read what that does to this product's feature list.** Verified stays,
safety signals on properties, a women's-review layer, AI itinerary planning —
these were all on the roadmap here, and all of them are now shipped by the
largest travel company in India, against inventory this project will never
have. `docs/15` recorded that ~80% of travel startups fail with "no
differentiation" the leading cause, and that AI integration alone is not a
moat. This is that warning arriving in person.

It does not weaken the conclusion of this document. It sharpens it:

| Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| Trips, inventory, supply | Nine operators, twenty years, 10% margins |
| **Safety signals, verified stays, women's reviews** | **Taken by MakeMyTrip, at 97,000-property scale** |
| AI planning and discovery | MakeMyTrip + OpenAI |
| **The family conversation** | **Still nobody** |

Anything on this site that competes with row two should be reconsidered, not
because it is badly built but because it is now a feature of an app with
national distribution and a supply relationship with every hotel in the
country. What survives is the row nobody is in.

### The behaviour data is stronger than the 2025 numbers suggested

- **"Women solo travel" searches hit a 15-year high in Q1 2026 on Google
  Trends**, with peak interest in **Mumbai, Delhi and Bengaluru** — three metro
  cities, which is direct support for Segment A in `docs/15`
- **Skyscanner 2026: 87% of Indians have travelled solo or are considering it,
  including 64% of women aged 30–50**
- Indian solo travel is projected to compound at **19.5% a year to 2033**
- Year on year: **train bookings 16.5×, bus 11×, flights 8.2×** — higher
  frequency and more regional movement
- **Over 80% of women's domestic leisure bookings are beach destinations**
  rather than hill stations, and **60%+ of their top international choices are
  visa-free**
- Gen Z women book mid-range stays (about half their bookings, up ~8×);
  millennial women skew premium, a third choosing luxury — the highest of any
  age group
- MakeMyTrip's own data: women book stays **50% further in advance** (15+ days)
  and index **16% higher on premium and branded properties** than men
- Women's labour force participation is up roughly **80% over six years**

The advance-booking figure is worth keeping. A woman who books two weeks
earlier than a man is a woman doing more planning — and possibly a woman who
needs the plan settled before she can get agreement to go.

### What this does not change

The category economics in the sections above. Growth projections describe the
whole solo travel market, not what a nine-operator bootstrapped category can
capture. WOW Club still took twenty years to reach 4,000 travellers a year.

## Testing the central claim: does anybody sell the family conversation?

Everything in this document turns on one assertion — that none of these
companies sells help with getting permission. It deserved more than a passing
check, so it got one on 29 Sep.

**The intended method failed.** Direct reads of `f5escapes.com`,
`wowclub.com` and `jugni.co.in` are blocked by this environment's network
egress proxy, so the operators' own pages could not be audited line by line.
What follows is a search probe, which is weaker, and the weakness is the point:
**the single load-bearing claim in this document has still not been verified
against the source.** Anyone with an unrestricted browser should spend twenty
minutes doing it.

**What the probe found: the demand is everywhere in content and nowhere in
products.**

There is a whole publishing genre on exactly this problem —
*"How to convince your Indian parents to let you travel"* (Holidify),
*"Dealing with travel-wary Indian parents"* (The Shooting Star),
*"How I convinced my parents to let me travel solo at 19"* (Tripoto),
*"I'm a middle-class working woman and this is how I convinced my parents"*
(Tripoto), *"How to convince your mom that going on a solo trip as a female is
completely safe in India"* (Tripoto). Written repeatedly, read enough to keep
being written.

And the advice in them is consistently the same: **share your complete
itinerary, include timings, give them contact numbers, call every night before
you sleep.** That is a description of the document this product now builds,
arrived at independently by people who had to solve it by hand.

**No operator product was found doing any of it.** The nine sell trips. The
bloggers write the workaround. Nobody has closed the gap between them.

This is better evidence than the original claim had. A problem that generates
this much content and no product is either genuinely unserved or genuinely
unmonetisable — and `docs/15`'s observed case, where a document was shown to
parents and won permission, is the one data point that distinguishes those.

## What could not be found

Recorded so nobody later mistakes a gap for a zero.

- **No women's-segment size for Veena World or Kesari.** Package counts and
  group sizes are public; traveller numbers and revenue are not.
- **No revenue or funding figures** for Jugni, F5 Escapes, The Flapper Life,
  Women on Clouds or Girls On The Go.
- **No profitability data for anyone.** Wandering Jane's 10% margin is the only
  published unit economic in the category.
- **No figure for women's share of Indian travel spend**, as already recorded in
  `docs/15`. Anyone quoting one is guessing.
- **No evidence any operator in this category has failed or closed.** Searched
  for it specifically. Low failure and low funding together suggest small,
  durable, founder-run businesses rather than a graveyard.

## What would change this conclusion

- An operator turning out to run materially better than 10% margins
- Any of the nine raising institutional money, which would mean an investor sees
  scale that this research does not
- Five conversations (Stage 3) in which women say they want a trip booked far
  more than they want help getting permission
