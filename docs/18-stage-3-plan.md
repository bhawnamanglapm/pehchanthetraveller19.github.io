Written 29 Sep 2026. `docs/15` says Stage 3 is *"started, stalled"* and gates
Stages 4, 9 and most of what remains. This is the plan to unstall it.

Four problems. Each gets a specific instrument, a number that counts as

## Problem 1 — the survey has almost no interview pipeline

Not just "n=3". Worse than that, and it was missed until now.

The survey's last question asks whether the respondent would join a future
conversation. Of the three real respondents: **No, No, Maybe.** The founder's
own test row says Yes.

**So the survey has produced, at most, one interviewable person.** It cannot
recruit the five conversations. Anyone planning to "go back to the
respondents" should stop planning that.

**Fix: recruit separately, and do not rely on the form for it.**

## Problem 2 — the survey will not fill itself

Four responses in six days, with nothing pointing at it. At that rate n=50
arrives in February.

**Fix: one piece of content, aimed at the highest-intent hook we found.**

Stage 2's probe turned up a whole publishing genre — *"How to convince your
Indian parents to let you travel"*, written repeatedly across Tripoto, Holidify
and The Shooting Star, and read enough to keep being written. That is a proven
format with proven demand, and **the founder now has something none of those
articles have: a real case where it worked, and the document that did it.**

The post writes itself:

> *My sister is a government employee. She still had to ask our parents before
> she could go to Andaman. Here is the document I made her, and here is what
> happened when she showed it to them.*

That is true, specific, and it is the only version of this article anyone can
write with an artefact attached. It ends with the survey link.

**Realistic arithmetic.** Survey completion from social traffic runs low —
assume 2–5%. Fifty responses therefore needs roughly **1,000–2,500 people to
see it**, which is one good post plus the Instagram bio changed to point at
`/women-and-travel/`, plus direct messages to everyone who has ever asked for
travel advice.

If the audience cannot produce 1,000 views, that is itself a finding, and it
belongs in Stage 9 rather than being treated as a failure of the survey.

## Problem 3 — the question a form cannot answer

Assumptions 4 and 5 are failing on survey data, and survey data is the wrong
instrument to kill them with. A checkbox saying "women-only group trip" tells
you nothing about why it was not ticked.

**Fix: five conversations, and one rule that decides whether they are worth
anything.**

> **Ask about the past, never about the future.**

"Would you use a service that…" produces politeness. "Tell me about the last
trip you wanted to take and didn't" produces facts. Every question below is
built that way, and the guide is in the next section.

## Problem 4 — nobody has ever paid

Assumption 2, completely untested, and no amount of interviewing fixes it.
People are generous with hypothetical money.

**Fix: name a price to a real person.** Design in its own section below.

# The interview guide

Five women, one hour each, at least three of them metro professionals aged
25–35 (Segment A — the only segment with more than one data point).

## Before you start

- **Never pitch.** The moment she knows what you want to hear, the data is
  gone. If asked, say you are researching how Indian women plan travel.
- **Ask about her life, not your idea.**
- **Talk less than a third of the time.** Silence is where the useful part
  arrives.
- **Write down what she says, not what you concluded.** Her words, including
  the awkward ones.
- **A compliment is not data.** "That sounds so useful" means nothing. Time,
  a referral, or money means something.

## The questions, in order

**1. Warm up — establish the real baseline.**
- When did you last travel for yourself, not for work or a wedding?
- Who decided where and when?
- Who did you go with?

**2. The last trip that did not happen. This is the most important question
   in the whole plan.**
- Tell me about a trip you wanted to take and didn't.
- What actually stopped it? Walk me through it.
- *(Say nothing. Let her fill the silence.)*
- Did you tell anyone at home you were thinking about it? What did they say?

**3. Permission, without using the word.**
- When you plan a trip, who do you tell, and when in the process?
- Has anyone at home ever asked you not to go somewhere? What happened?
- What would they want to know before they were comfortable?
- Has anyone in your family ever needed convincing? How did you do it?

**4. Solo, asked honestly** — this is the one that resolves assumption 4.
- If everything were arranged, paid for and safe, would you rather go alone or
  with people?
- *(Then, whatever she says:)* Why?
- Have you ever travelled alone? What was the part you did not expect?

**5. Women-only** — assumption 5.
- Have you heard of trips that are only for women?
- Would you go on one? What appeals, what doesn't?
- *(If negative:)* What would have to be true for you to consider it?

**6. Planning, and what it costs her.**
- Walk me through planning your last trip. What did you actually do first?
- How long did that take?
- What was the most annoying part?
- Has anyone ever planned a trip *for* you? How was it?

**7. The artefact test — show, do not describe.**
- *(Hand over the Andaman document. Say nothing about it.)*
- What is this? What would you use it for?
- *(Then:)* Would you show this to anyone?
- *(Only if she says yes:)* Who, and why them?

**8. Close.**
- Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
- Who else should I talk to?

## What counts as a signal

| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| She names a specific trip that didn't happen, and who stopped it | "Safety is a concern for women" |
| She describes convincing someone, unprompted | "My family is quite supportive" |
| She asks to keep the document | "This looks really nice" |
| She offers to introduce someone | "Good luck with it" |
| She asks what it costs | "I would definitely use this" |

**The last row is the one to listen for.** Nobody asks the price of something
they do not want.

# The price test

## The number: ₹2,500

Derived, not guessed. From `docs/17`: operators charge ₹48,999–₹58,000 a trip
at roughly a 10% margin, so a tour operator earns about **₹5,000 of gross
margin per traveller**. A document priced at **₹2,500** is half an operator's
margin, about **4% of a ₹63,000 trip**, and carries no cost of goods at all.

It is also high enough to hurt. A price nobody hesitates over teaches nothing.

## How to run it

**Trigger:** the next person who asks for help planning a trip. From
`docs/15`, this has happened at least once already, unprompted.

**Script**, to be said without apology or discount:

> *I make these properly now — a full itinerary with the day plan, both
> journeys with connection times, where you're staying and a real budget. It's
> ₹2,500. I'll have it to you in three days.*

Then stop talking.

**Faster version, if nobody asks.** Post the Andaman document publicly as proof
of work, with the same offer and the same price. That is a real test, costs
nothing, and the audience is already there.

**Record three numbers, nothing else:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Asked** | People who wanted help |
| **Quoted** | People who were told ₹2,500 |
| **Paid** | People who paid |

UPI and a note. No gateway, no entity, no invoice — none of that is needed
below the ₹20 lakh GST threshold within one state (`docs/15`, Constraints).

**Resolution:** 20 quoted, fewer than 2 paid → **assumption 2 is dead**, and
this is an audience business that monetises some other way. 2 or more paid →
there is a business, and its price is known.

# What "resolved" means, per assumption

Stage 1's exit rule requires assumptions 1, 4 and 5 resolved *in writing*.
Here is what would do it.

| | Assumption | Resolved when |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Family permission is the real blocker | At n=30, half or more report family influence — **or**, in five interviews, three or more describe a specific trip shaped by someone else's approval |
| **4** | She wants to travel **solo** | Five interviews answer question 4. If four or more choose company over solitude, **the word comes out of the positioning** and the product becomes about permission, not solitude |
| **5** | She wants **women-only** | If three or more react negatively or indifferently in question 5, stop treating it as a feature. It stays a fact about the trips, not a pitch |
| **2** | Anyone will pay | The price test above |
