# What the survey actually says (n=3)

Read 29 Sep 2026. The Women & Travel survey has **four responses**. One is the
founder's own test entry, so **n=3 real respondents**, up from 2 when
`docs/15` was written.

No raw responses, contact details or timestamps are recorded here or anywhere
in this repository. Only aggregates and anonymised descriptions.

Three people cannot validate a product. They can tell you which of your
assumptions is already in trouble — and two of them are.

## Finding 1 — nobody in this data travels solo

The founder is the only solo traveller in the sheet. Of the three real
respondents: one has been solo once or twice, one has only been alone inside
her own city, one does not want to go at all.

Asked which trip they would most likely **book**:

- Mixed group trip
- Family trip
- Friends trip

**Zero chose a solo trip. Zero chose a women-only group trip** — the only
person who picked that was the founder.

This is the sharpest thing in the data, because the product is currently built
and named as a *solo* travel confidence engine. At n=3 it is not proof. But the
one respondent closest to the stated target user — metro, 25–30, working,
single, never travelled alone outside her city — says plainly that she has
*"safety concerns"*, *"don't know how to plan"* and **"don't like solo
travelling"**, and would book a friends trip.

The confidence engine may be right. The word *solo* may be wrong. What all
three want is to travel **accompanied but not chaperoned** — and to have the
planning and the verification done for them.

## Finding 2 — the family thesis holds, and got stronger

Every respondent reports family influence over the decision. None answered
"none".

Asked how likely their family is to support a 5-day trip with a company where
they don't know everyone (1–5): **3, 1, 2. Mean 2.0.** Family is not a soft
worry in this data; it is the gate.

Asked what would make family comfortable, **"Complete itinerary" and "Hotel
details" appear in three of four responses** — more than any other answer.
That is exactly what the Family Pack sends. Keep it.

> averaged — a 25–30 metro freelancer, a 51–60 married woman in a town, and a

| Live location sharing | 4.3 |
| Trip tracking | 4.3 |
| Police/hospital information | 4.3 |
| Destination safety information | 4.3 |
| Verified hotels | 4.0 |
| Background-verified travellers | 4.0 |
| Local women contacts | 4.0 |
| Travel insurance | 4.0 |
| Female trip leader | 3.7 |
| Women-only groups | 3.7 |
| SOS button | 3.3 |
| Female drivers where available | **2.7** |

The two top-scoring features are both *family-facing*, and both are already
built (check-ins, the I'm-OK button, the Family Pack). Good.

The four lowest are the gendered-segregation features — women-only groups,
female trip leader, female drivers — and the panic button. **"Women-only" is
the weakest idea in the survey, and it is the idea the whole category is built
on.** Worth noticing before building any of it.

## Finding 4 — willingness to pay is stated, not demonstrated

Would you pay extra for a women-focused service with safety and support?
**Definitely, Definitely, Probably.** Four of four positive including the
founder.

And yet: two say a 4–5 day domestic trip budget "depends heavily on
destination", one says under ₹10,000. Two of three would rather have *lower
price with basic comfort* or say it depends, rather than paying up.

Stated willingness to pay with no number attached is not demand. Nothing in
this sheet is evidence anyone will pay. That needs a price test, not another
survey question.

## Finding 5 — planning is a real, measurable cost

Time spent planning a trip: 5–10 hours, 2–5 hours, 2–5 hours.

Hardest parts, in order of repetition: choosing a destination (2), budgeting
(2), finding safe hotels, creating the itinerary, finding local transport,
understanding local safety, booking everything, visa/documentation.

Asked what they'd prefer: *recommendations and I book myself*, *everything
planned for me*, *a combination depending on the trip*. **Nobody wants pure
DIY.** The product currently gives them pure DIY — a profile, a checklist and
a set of tools. That is a gap between what is built and what is asked for.

## What this does to the three hypotheses in `docs/15`

| | Hypothesis | Status now |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Metro professional, 25–35, blocked by leave not money | **Strengthened, n=2.** Both new-ish respondents are metro, 25–30, working, single, travel 1–2×/year. Both name *time* — "limited annual paid leave", "timing" — not money. The founder is the only one who said money. |
| **B** | Older married woman, smaller town, needs spousal approval | **Unchanged, still n=1.** No second instance appeared. |
| **C** | NRI daughter | **Still zero evidence.** Pure conjecture. It should stop being listed as a segment until someone appears. |

Segment A is the only one growing, and it is now the only one with more than a
single data point. If a choice has to be made before n=50, A is the only
defensible one.

## What changes, and what does not

**Do not change yet:**
- The Family Pack, check-ins and I'm-OK — top-rated, and already built
- Complete itinerary + hotel details as the family artefact — most-requested
- Verified accommodation and verified drivers — consistently 4+

**Question seriously:**
- The word **solo** in the positioning. Nobody here is solo, and the closest-fit
  respondent explicitly dislikes it
- **Women-only groups and female trip leaders** — the lowest-rated ideas here
- Giving people **tools instead of a plan**. All three want something done for
  them, in different amounts

**Do not conclude anything about:**
- Price. Three "yes I'd pay" answers with no number is not a finding
- Segment B or C. One and zero respondents respectively

## The next honest step

arrives. A survey nobody is driven to does not fill up on its own.

   Three of the five should be the closest match to Segment A. The single

2. **Then drive the form** — Instagram bio to `/women-and-travel/`, and one post
