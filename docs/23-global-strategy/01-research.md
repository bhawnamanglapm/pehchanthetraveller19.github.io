# 01 — Research: the market, and what it means for us

Searched 7 Nov 2026. **Read the source column before trusting a number.**
- Market-research firms disagree with each other by tens of billions.
- Many travel "statistics" pages are blogs quoting other blogs.
- Surveys commissioned by companies serve those companies.

A figure marked *weak* is here to show a direction, not to be quoted on the site.

---

## Finding 1: solo travel is large, and the "women are X%" figures are shaky

| Claim | Figure | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global solo travel market, 2025 | $549.78bn | [Grand View Research](https://www.marketresearch.com/Grand-View-Research-v4060/Solo-Travel-44356786) | Paid report, summary only |
| Same | $558.07bn | [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/solo-travel-market-115110) | Same |
| Same | $489.32bn | [TechSci Research](https://www.techsciresearch.com/report/solo-travel-market/28635.html) | Same |
| "Women are ~65% of solo travellers" | 65% | Blog citations of an unnamed "Georgetown" report | **Weak, unverified. Don't use** |
| Women 50+ in the US and Canada: solo is 61% of their market | US$152bn | [JourneyWoman](https://journeywoman.com/news/journeywoman-releases-new-study-invisible-no-more-the-ageless-adventuress-how-women-50-are-fuelling-travels-billion-dollar-boom/) | Industry study, North America only |

**What it means:** leaving "solo women" isn't escaping a small market. It's escaping a crowded one. The case for
going broader is that **the mechanism we've designed (reports, verdicts, profile, document) works for any
traveller**, and limiting it to one group throws away most of its data.

## Finding 2: AI trip planning is saturated, and the big players are already in it

- **Mindtrip** launched in-chat flight booking with Sabre and PayPal on 6 May 2026. **Layla** books through
  Skyscanner. **Wanderlog** is the collaborative organiser. **Gemini** does research with Maps.
  **Booking.com** and **Tripadvisor** have their own assistants.
  ([fast.io roundup](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-for-travel-planning-2026/),
  [2026 deep dive](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/culture/2026-05-16-ai-travel-itinerary-planning-2026-mindtrip-layla-wonderplan-roam-around-vacay-guidegeek-booking-ai-hopper-deep-dive.en),
  [Layla's tier list](https://layla.ai/blog/destinations/ai-trip-planners-tier-list), [Stippl](https://app.stippl.io/blog/best-ai-travel-planner-2026).
  Most of these are vendor-written, so treat their rankings as opinion.)
- The common criticism is accuracy: live prices that aren't live, and wrong connections.

**What it means: don't build another itinerary planner.** It's the most crowded category in travel right now, and
the incumbents have booking inventory we never will. Every planner tells you *what to do*. None of them tells you
*whether it will match what you expect*.

## Finding 3: content sites are losing traffic to AI answers

- Heads of SEO at four large lifestyle publishers report Google referrals down **30–50%** since AI Overviews
  launched in May 2024 ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/lifestyle-publishers-rewrite-the-seo-playbook-for-ai-driven-search/)).
- The travel blog *The Planet D* reportedly lost half its traffic in the months after the rollout, per Bloomberg
  as relayed by secondary sources ([AdExchanger](https://adexchanger.com/publishers/the-ai-search-reckoning-is-dismantling-open-web-traffic-and-publishers-may-never-recover)).
  *Reports of what happened next conflict. Weak.*
- Tripadvisor is pivoting towards a daily-use app as search traffic falls
  ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/tripadvisor-pivots-to-daily-app-as-google-ai-threatens-search-traffic/)).
- **Culture Trip** raised about $155m, pivoted to selling trips, cut 80% of staff in 2022, and sold its site and
  tours business to U.S. News ([Skift](https://skift.com/2024/02/29/culture-trips-final-pivot-sells-to-u-s-news-after-raising-155-million)).

**What it means:** destination guides are what AI summarises best, so they are the weakest asset to build a global
site on. **What AI can't summarise is data that exists nowhere else.** First-party numbers from travellers ("6 of 11
first-timers said it lived up to it") are something an AI answer would have to *cite*, not replace.

## Finding 4: the gap between expectation and reality is real, widespread and unmeasured per destination

| Market | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Australia | 35% booked a dream trip inspired by influencers. **57% felt let down by the real thing** (n=1,000, Insure&Go) | [TravelTalk](https://traveltalkmedia.com.au/?p=201946) |
| UK | More than half have been disappointed when a destination didn't match the images online. 55% say photos or videos made a place look different | [Holiday Extras](https://www.holidayextras.com/travel-news/digital-dissonance.html) |
| US | **Fewer than half said their last vacation lived up to expectations** (n=2,000, Exodus Travels and OnePoll) | [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/many-americans-disappointed-with-most-recent-vacation-survey-finds) |
| General | Commentary on why trips disappoint: feeds freeze moments, and people remember whole trips | [Kiwi.com](https://www.kiwi.com/stories/why-so-many-trips-disappoint-the-gap-between-instagram-and-reality/) |

All three surveys were commissioned by travel companies and use different questions. They can't be compared with
each other, but **they point the same way in three countries.**

**What it means:** this is a global problem, not a women's or an Indian problem. Every survey measures it
*nationally, once*. **No one I found measures it per destination, per type of traveller, continuously.** That's
the gap (`02`).

## Finding 5: people don't trust reviews, so they turn to strangers on Reddit

- Tripadvisor rejected or removed **2.7m fraudulent reviews in 2024**, up from about 2m in 2023. 54% of the fraud
  was businesses boosting themselves, and 214,000 reviews were AI-generated
  ([Tripadvisor transparency report](https://tripadvisor.mediaroom.com/2025-03-18-Tripadvisors-2025-Transparency-Report-reveals-strong-review-submissions-and-improved-fraud-detection),
  [CNBC via NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/heres-how-many-fake-reviews-tripadvisor-found-on-its-website-in-2024/6277840/)).
- Travellers add "reddit" to searches for "unfiltered opinions" that counterbalance AI summaries
  ([10 Minutes Hotel, Nov 2025](https://en.10minhotel.com/2025/11/21/social-medias-growing-role-in-travel-discovery),
  [Afar](https://www.afar.com/magazine/why-reddit-is-a-great-travel-planning-tool)). Gen Z and millennials rate
  Reddit as more trustworthy than Google's first page. Older groups don't
  ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/399516/None)).
- The standard criticism of Reddit: you're trusting someone you know nothing about.

**What it means:** people want real experience, and they also want to know *whose* experience it is. **A lens ("people
like you") plus a sample size ("11 of them") answers exactly that criticism of Reddit.** Fraud is the main threat to
any report-based product. The integrity mechanics in `docs/11` and `docs/12` aren't optional.

## Finding 6: there are disruption and entry-rule tools, but for booked trips or for businesses

- **TripIt Pro Risk Alerts** (data from Riskline) push alerts for weather, unrest, strikes, outages and airport
  incidents on trips you've already booked ([TripIt](https://www.tripit.com/web/blog/news-culture/tripit-pro-risk-alerts),
  [help](https://help.tripit.com/en/support/solutions/articles/103000280834-risk-alerts)).
- **Sitata** monitors news and social media with AI. **Strike Tracker** watches strikes for your trips
  ([Sitata](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id963253974), [Strike Tracker](https://mwm.ai/apps/strike-tracker-work-travel/6751948513)).
- **Sherpa** offers a free consumer checker of entry requirements by passport and destination, and sells the same
  data to travel companies ([Sherpa](https://joinsherpa.com/products/travel-requirements)).
- **Rules really are volatile.** Even news sources disagree about whether the EU's ETIAS starts in late 2026 or slips
  to 2027 ([Visas News](https://visasnews.com/en/despite-a-rocky-ees-rollout-etias-is-still-expected-by-late-2026/),
  [InsideFlyer](https://www.insideflyer.com/posts/etias-launch-delayed-again-now-expected-in-2027/),
  [Connexion](https://www.connexionfrance.com/news/delay-forecast-for-eu-travel-authorisation-scheme-after-website-changes/808384)).

**What it means:** "what changed" is covered *for people who've already booked* (TripIt) and *for businesses*
(Sherpa). The gap is at the **deciding** stage: before booking, while you're still asking "should I go?". The radar
already sweeps government and policy news. That is its consumer use, **once a person verifies each item**.

## Finding 7: personality-matching quizzes exist, and they're gimmicks

The Spot For Me, Woofound (2012, $1.18m angel round), Hilton's "Holiday Matchmaker" (Big Five traits), Tripanion
and Baryl all match people to places from a quiz
([The Spot For Me](https://www.newswire.com/news/the-spot-for-me-finds-travel-destinations-for-people-based-on-their-20871315),
[Woofound](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/woofound-launches-app-to-match-people-to-places-based-on-personality/),
[Hilton](https://www.ft.lk/travel-tourism/hilton-launches-new-holiday-matchmaker-fb-app/27-449105)).
A commenter on a newer one called a quiz "too reductive for something as personal as travel"
([Rakun](https://hunted.space/product/rakun-sensory-sanctuary-quiz)).

**What it means: the Profile alone is not a differentiator.** A quiz that outputs a destination is a toy. A profile
that **filters real reports from people with the same answers** is different, because the output is evidence, not
a horoscope. **Never market the Profile as a quiz.**

## Finding 8: groups and families plan together, and tools exist for voting but not for persuading

- 57% of US families planned multigenerational trips, and children increasingly shape decisions (2025 US Family
  Travel Survey, [Family Travel Association](https://www.familytravel.org/?p=58152)).
- 72% of respondents to a Mindtrip survey travel with family across age groups. Mindtrip launched group chat, and
  Kayak built Trip Huddle for voting ([Globetrender](https://globetrender.com/?p=71548),
  [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Kayak-Trip-Huddle-tool)).

**What it means:** voting tools solve "which of these do we pick". **The Trip Document solves "convince the people
who have to say yes"**, whether that's parents, a partner, a group or a manager. That need isn't Indian or female
only. It's anyone who has to justify a trip to someone else. `docs/17` found no operator selling it.

## Finding 9: the first market to focus on — Indian outbound travel is large and growing

- About **29m Indian outbound trips in 2025**, up about 10% on 2024
  ([Wesgro, 2026](https://www.wesgro.co.za/uploads/files/Wesgro-Research_India-Tourism-Market-Insights-2026.pdf)).
- Forecasts for 2030: **47m** ([Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/tourism-flows-in-india/report)) and
  **50m+** ([CAPA India](https://asianaviation.com/capa-india-says-country-to-have-50-million-annual-outbound-travellers-by-fy2030/)).
  The sources define "trips" and "travellers" differently.

**What it means:** a global brand still needs **one place where its numbers fill up first**. The founder has been
to five international destinations (`docs/06`), and those are among the corridors Indian outbound travellers use.
That's where the first verdict cards can cross their thresholds (`05`).

## Finding 10: accessibility is a real lens, not a charity page

US travellers with disabilities spend about **$50bn a year**, with 25.6m of them taking 77m trips over 2022–24
(Open Doors Organization and Mandala Research,
[PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/50-billion-in-annual-spending-highlights-impact-of-travelers-with-disabilities-302321017.html)).
Market-research estimates for "accessible travel" range from $80bn to more than $1tn, depending on the definition.
*Ignore the range.*

**What it means:** "is it accessible for someone like me" is exactly the question generic reviews answer worst. It's a
natural second or third lens. It's also **sensitive data**, so collect it only with consent (`05`).
